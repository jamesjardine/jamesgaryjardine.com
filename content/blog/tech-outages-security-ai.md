Have you noticed the recent tech outages over the last few weeks?  Of course you have, it is all over, well everything. 

It isn't uncommon for tech to have some outages, but the past 2 weeks have felt different. It seems like everything sort of hit at once with 2 big events happening so close to each other:
* The Microsoft outage that took down email and Teams world wide
* The FAA issue that caused a full US grounding

Add on issues immediately following that:
* Multiple banks having issues
* Apple
* NSA communications
* Major airlines

As I mentioned, tech issues happen all the time, just usually not on top of each other or so wide spread. We have seen a number of times where a **region** may go down for a small period of time, but that limited scope minimizes the impact. So put a bunch of these events close to each other tied with social media screaming the end of the world, people start to notice. 

## Cyber Attacks?
The first thing a lot of people, especially the security industry, will point to is the potential for cyber attacks. It can be really tough sometimes to say whether something was related to a cyber attack or not when looking in from the outside. The companies will all say it was not a cyber attack. Of course, that is what the default reaction is always going to be. However, unless something comes out to prove it wasn't, it is hard to really say it was. 

## AI? ##
The next obvious question is if AI is somehow the question. Well, maybe that is the first question for most people these days. While cyber attacks can sometimes be tricky to prove (unless you have strong evidence) AI adds a different level of complexity. 

I think with AI there are different perspectives that could be reviewed:
* AI Directly
* AI Indirectly

### AI Directly ###
Direct AI involvement, in my mind, would be more so of either AI making direct changes to a system that were not correct. This could be a developer agent that has access to make changes to production systems directly, or agents that have just gone rogue and found a way to change something they shouldn't. 

We have seen multiple examples of agents going rogue and breaking out of sandboxes to go attach other systems. 

We also have a lot of companies using AI for development of their software. There have been multiple examples of agents deleting production databases and causing disruptions. There is also the case that if AI is writing the code, who is reviewing that to ensure it is right before pushing to production?

### AI Indirectly ###
Indirect AI involvement is more around the repercussions of AI adoption at companies. We have seen lots of companies reducing their head count to make room for their AI goals. By putting more focus on AI doing things, there has been a removal of people to watch it. Often times this will remove a lot of tribal knowledge from a company and its product lines leading to errors occurring. 

This indirect involvement is harder to quantify because companies are not going to just say that an event happened because of cuts made to bring in AI. It sort of goes against the narrative. For an AI Direct involvement, it is easy to just defend that AI is new and mistakes will happen. Notice how that still supports AI. Indirect can still show support for AI, but it carries with it the negative effect on real people that more will empathize with as a negative for AI. 

## Something Else ##
Well I mentioned security and AI, but those aren't the only players in town. It could be that issues just happen. In one example, there was mention of a bad router (that can happen). In another it was an update made months before that their test cases did not test for a specific scenario, that just happened to happen months later. 

## Wrap Up ##
As I mentioned earlier on, there are outages all the time, you just don't hear about most of them. Do I think it is the end of the world, no. But hey, if I am wrong, I guess it won't matter anyway. 

It is rare that we see large events, like a full FAA ground stop, or a vendor like Microsoft having a world wide outage. Those things will generate some headlines, even if you were the one actually getting some focus time to get real work done while not having to respond to 500 emails an hour. 

However, these events just create a microscope around other events to make things seem more uncommon then they are. I think most people would be really surprised to see how often sites they use have some level of outage, it is just typically smaller and media didn't tell you that you should be upset about it.
